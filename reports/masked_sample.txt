4. RE-IDENTIFICATION AFTER MASKING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Group sizes on the quasi-identifiers. Larger groups hide each person
among more people.

Before : date_of_birth, address_postal, income   (unparseable component in 0.0%)
After  : date_of_birth, income, created_date   (unparseable component in 0.0%)
Unparseable quasi-identifiers share an 'unknown' signature, which
groups them and overstates their protection.
The model changes with masking: postal code goes with the suppressed
address, DOB and income are generalised, created_date joins at year
granularity. The after set covers every released quasi-identifier,
including unmasked columns - scoring only masked ones would flatter.

GROUP SIZE          BEFORE     AFTER
k=1 (unique)          3682      1761
k=2                      0      1288
k=3-5                    0       633
k>5                      0         0

Uniquely re-identifiable: 100.0% -> 47.8%

Achieved by generalising quasi-identifiers, not by masking direct
ones. Masking names and emails alone leaves this figure unchanged.

1761 records remain unique on the modelled post-mask
quasi-identifiers.
k-anonymity is a risk indicator, not proof of anonymity: it measures
uniqueness only over the quasi-identifiers chosen, says nothing about
attribute disclosure within a group, and an attacker may hold
attributes not modelled here. The extract is pseudonymous, not
anonymous, and remains personal data under GDPR.
