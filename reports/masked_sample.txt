3. LEAKED PII IN FREE TEXT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Part 2 found emails, phones and SSNs inside the address field.
Replacing the whole field removes them. Partial masking - keeping a
city or postal code - would have left every one of them in place.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. RE-IDENTIFICATION AFTER MASKING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Group sizes on the quasi-identifiers, before and after. Larger groups
mean each person is hidden among more people.

Before : date_of_birth, address_postal, income
After  : date_of_birth, income, created_date
The two sets differ because masking removed dimensions. The after
set covers every released attribute designated a quasi-identifier,
including columns left unmasked - scoring only the masked ones
would flatter the result.

GROUP SIZE          BEFORE     AFTER
k=1 (unique)          3684      1775
k=2                      0      1230
k=3-5                    0       673
k>5                      0         6

Uniquely re-identifiable: 100.0% -> 48.2%

Achieved by generalising the quasi-identifiers, not by masking the
direct identifiers: dropping the postal code with the address, coarsening
the birth date to a year and banding income. Masking names and emails
alone would have left the figure unchanged.

1775 records remain unique on the released attributes.
k-anonymity is a risk indicator, not proof of anonymity: it measures
uniqueness only over the quasi-identifiers chosen, says nothing about
attribute disclosure within a group, and an attacker may hold
attributes not modelled here. The extract is pseudonymous, not
anonymous, and remains personal data under GDPR.
